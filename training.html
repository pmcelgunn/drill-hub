🏋️ Pre-Season Training 4-week at-home program · ages 9-12 · 15-30 min/day

Week 1Week 2Week 3Week 4🌊 Recovery🔧 No-Gear Version📚 Drill Hub
How to use: 5 short workouts + 1 active-recovery day + 1 full rest day each week, for 4 weeks. Each session is 15-30 minutes: dynamic warm-up → the day's work → a ball-skill finisher → cool-down. Do the days in order or fit them to your week. Built for motivated 9-12 year olds from real, full-text sports-science research (studies cited inside). Underlined drill names link to the full Drill Hub card.
🔄
Warm-Up & Cool-Down (every session)
Dynamic before · static after
▶

Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — every session, ~4 min

  • RAISE (~60s): jog the trail loop or in place + 2 build-up runs (jog → 75% → 90%). Break a light sweat.
  • ACTIVATE + MOBILIZE (~90s): 5 walking lunges w/ torso twist · 8 leg swings each way (front-back + side) · 1 inchworm walkout ×3 · 8 glute bridges · arm circles + band pull-aparts if you have one
  • POTENTIATE (~60s): 3 A-skips + 2 short accelerations (10 yd) + 2 change-of-direction cuts at speed
Why
  • Never static-stretch before speed work — it made kids 2.5% slower and cut jumps 6.3% (Paradisis 2014). Move, don't hold.

Cool-Down + Stretch — every session, ~3 min

  • ~60s easy: walk it out, easy ball toss, breathing down
  • ~90s static holds (20-30s each): quads · hamstrings · calves · hip flexors · shoulders/chest. Flexibility comes from doing this a little, often (Flexibility-window meta 2022).
Why
  • A cooldown winds you down and builds flexibility over the weeks — it does NOT 'flush' soreness (Van Hooren 2018). Static stretching belongs HERE, after.

Week 1

LEARN — teach every movement, mostly easy, set your baseline
🚀
Day 1 · Speed & Acceleration
20-25 min · Week 1
▶
Short sprints + mechanics = the #1 speed builder for your age. Kids' speed lives in the first 10-20 yards (Frontiers 2025).

Warm-up

  • Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — tap for the 4-min routine

Feet quicks + technical (5 min)

  • Feet quicks: front-back, side-side, scissors — 3 × 10s each
  • A-March → A-Skip → A-Run, ×2 each + 10-yd run-out
  • Arm-action: kneeling arm swings 2 × 15s (fast, relaxed hands)

Sprint-oriented plyos (~~40 foot contacts)

  • Pogo hops 2 × 15s (quiet, springy ankles)
  • Broad jump ×4 — stick the landing heel-to-toe
  • Snap-downs ×5 (load fast, freeze low) — this is deceleration
Coach for
  • Land on the balls of the feet, chest tall, knees over toes
  • Grass or turf only — never concrete (plyo surface rule)

Speed: 6 × 10 yd (walk back, full recovery)

  • Varied starts: standing, kneeling, on-your-belly, falling-forward, lateral
  • Walk all the way back between reps — every sprint is 100% and fully rested (~60s per 10 yд)
  • Race a sibling/parent from a stagger to make it a competition
Coach for
  • FRESH sprints only — a tired sprint builds nothing
  • Push the ground BEHIND you, arms drive cheek-to-cheek
Skill finisher (4 min): 9-Spot Hands + 10 Rapid-Catch reps — hands away from body, tuck every ball.

Cool-down

  • Cool-Down + Stretch — tap for the routine

Day 2 · Strength A — Squat/Push/Carry
20-25 min · Week 1
▶
Technique-first strength. Pre-puberty gains are neural — coordination and force, not size (AAP 2020). Master the move, THEN add light load.

Warm-up

  • Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — tap for the 4-min routine

Squat pattern — 1-2 sets × 8-10, bodyweight/PVC only

  • PVC/goblet squat to a box (sit to the box, stand tall)
  • Progress: goblet squat holding a NO added load — pattern & bodyweight
  • Split-squat / lunge ×8 each leg
Coach for
  • Chest up, knees track over toes, full depth you can control
  • Barbell = an empty bar or PVC to learn the pattern ONLY — no plates in this block

Push pattern — 1-2 sets × 8-10, bodyweight/PVC only

  • Push-ups (hands on a box → floor as you get strong)
  • Band or light-KB overhead press ×8-10
  • Optional: KB floor press ×8
Coach for
  • Body in a straight line, ribs down
  • Slow down, full range — no bouncing

Carry + core (3-4 min)

  • Suitcase carry (one KB) ×20 yd each side
  • Farmer carry (two KBs) ×20 yd
  • Plank 2 × 20-30s + dead bug ×8/side
Coach for
  • Stand tall, brace the belly, don't lean
Skill finisher (4 min): Backpedal mechanics ×5 + towel/flag pulls — eyes on the belt, rip straight down.

Cool-down

  • Cool-Down + Stretch — tap for the routine

Day 3 · Agility & Reaction
20-25 min · Week 1
▶
Change-of-direction and reaction are MOST trainable before puberty (COD effect size 0.62 pre-PHV, Ramirez-Campillo 2023). Planned first, reactive later.

Warm-up

  • Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — tap for the 4-min routine

Lateral plyos + prep (5 min)

  • Skater bounds ×6/side (stick each landing)
  • Lateral line hops 2 × 15s
  • Lateral pogos forward + back, 5 yd

Change of direction — planned cuts, walk-through then 75%

  • 5-5-10: 5 yd out, touch, 5 yd back, touch, 10 yd finish — both directions
  • Pro-agility with a PAUSE at each turn (teach the plant, then speed up)
  • Zig-zag flag lane: one hard cut per cone, burst out of each
Coach for
  • Plant OUTSIDE your frame, hips DROP, then explode
  • Fewest steps to redirect wins (Footwork King rule)

Reaction (Wk3-4 emphasis)

  • Mirror drill: match a partner's shuffles for 5s, then chase on the break
  • Cut-on-cue: parent points or calls a direction late — cut the opposite way and go
  • Color-call sprint: 3 colored markers, sprint-touch the one that's called
Coach for
  • Read the body, not the feet — hips don't lie
  • React, don't guess
Skill finisher (4 min): Ball-security circuit (arm switches, hop-and-switch) + one Move-Menu move per rep.

Cool-down

  • Cool-Down + Stretch — tap for the routine

Day 4 · Engine — Circuit Game Day
18-25 min · Week 1
▶
Your CrossFit/Hyrox day — the fun one. Short work:rest circuits build real fitness in game format (School-HIIT meta 2022). Kept light so it never wrecks the speed/skill days.

Warm-up

  • Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — tap for the 4-min routine

Engine — 10-min AMRAP

  • AMRAP (as many rounds as possible) — count your rounds, beat it next week:
  • • 8 bear-crawl yards
  • • 6 light goblet squats (NO added load — pattern & bodyweight)
  • • 4 monkey-bar traverses (or 20s dead hang)
  • • 20s jump rope
  • • 4 broad jumps
  • OR EMOM (every minute on the minute, rest the rest of the minute): min1 = 20s rope sprint · min2 = 6 KB deadlifts · min3 = 8 push-ups + a hang
Coach for
  • Form never breaks for a faster round — light load, clean reps
  • Score = total rounds (AMRAP) or rounds survived (EMOM). Log it, race yourself.

Trail-loop finisher (optional, 4 min)

  • 6-8 × ~20s hard runs up the 0.2-mi loop, walk back full recovery
  • This — not slow laps — is what builds a kid's engine (needs >80% effort)
Skill finisher (4 min): Towel snaps ×10 + QB 1-2-3 to 3 targets (or 15 spirals to a partner/wall).

Cool-down

  • Cool-Down + Stretch — tap for the routine
💪
Day 5 · Strength B — Pull + Mobility
22-28 min · Week 1
▶
Pull-focused strength (grip = flag pulling) + a longer mobility block. Monkey bars and pull-up bar are ideal, safe, self-limiting strength for kids (AAP 2020).

Warm-up

  • Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — tap for the 4-min routine

Pull pattern — 1-2 sets × 8-10, bodyweight/PVC only

  • Pull-up bar: dead hangs → band-assisted pull-ups → negatives (lower slow for 5s)
  • Monkey-bar traverse — progress by distance, never by adding weight
  • Band rows ×10-12 (squeeze the shoulder blades)
Coach for
  • Grip and posture here = pulling flags in games
  • Soft ground under the bar, parent spotting

Single-leg + power (Wk3-4)

  • Step-ups to the box ×8/leg
  • Single-leg balance + reach ×5/leg (ankle/knee stability for cutting)
  • Wk3-4 only: 2-hand KB swing ×10 (hinge must be clean first — sub-maximal, no ballistic max)
Coach for
  • Swing is a HINGE, not a squat — snap the hips, arms are ropes

Mobility flow (~6 min)

  • World's greatest stretch ×5/side · deep squat pry-and-hold 3×20s · 90/90 hip switches ×8 · thoracic openers ×8/side · ankle wall-rocks ×10/side (from Speed Blueprint mobility)
  • PVC pass-throughs ×10 + PVC overhead squat ×8 (teaches the overhead/squat pattern — no load)
  • Optional CheatCODE foot micro-dose: towel curls 60s + toe-point pogos 2×20s (ankle stiffness) — stop at ANY heel pain (Sever's window is 9-12)
Skill finisher (4 min): Route tree in the yard — hitch/slant/out, snap the break, eyes last out.

Cool-down

  • Cool-Down + Stretch — tap for the routine

Coach for

Recovery, Testing & Safety

Combine Test Battery (Week 1 baseline + Week 4 retest)

  • 10-yard sprint (phone timer, start on first movement) — best of 2
  • Pro-agility 5-10-5 (cones 5 yds apart) — best of 2
  • Standing broad jump (tape measure) — best of 3
  • Max push-ups in 40s · max flag-belt jukes through a 4-cone zig-zag in 30s (fun)

🌊 Active Recovery Day (1×/week)

Easy movement clears fatigue better than sitting still (easy swim cleared lactate 68% vs 20%, PMC3931336). Pick ONE:
  • POOL (best): 15-20 min easy swim / tread / aqua-jog at ~50-60% — ADULT IN THE WATER ZONE, ARM'S REACH, ALWAYS. The pool is the #1 hazard in this whole program.
  • BIKE: 15-20 min easy neighborhood spin (low-impact aerobic volume)
  • TRAIL: 15-20 min easy walk/jog of the loop + the Mobility flow
  • Add: CheatCODE foot + balance + VOR micro-dose (5 min) and light stretching

😴 Full Rest Day (1×/week — this IS training)

  • SLEEP is the #1 recovery tool — target 9-12 hours/night. ≥8h cut injury odds 68% in young athletes (Milewski). It beats every gadget combined and costs nothing.
  • Play freely, hydrate, eat well. No structured workout.
  • AAP: at least 1-2 rest days/week; total organized-sport hours in a week should stay under your age in years.

The Recovery-Tech Truth (for parents)

  • Theragun / massage gun: OPTIONAL comfort tool. Parent-run, light setting, 1-2 min on big muscles (quads, calves, glutes) if sore. Avoid joints, spine, neck, head. No evidence it boosts youth performance — it just feels good.
  • TENS units (Chirp): SKIP for healthy kids. It's a pain-management device with zero recovery/performance evidence in healthy children. Only if a doctor prescribes it for a specific issue.
  • Muscle scraping (Sidekick/IASTM): SKIP for this age. Evidence is adults-only, low-quality, and short-lived; a growing kid with open growth plates is the wrong candidate. Clinician-only if ever.
  • What actually works: sleep, dynamic warm-ups, easy active-recovery movement, consistent post-session stretching, and not over-scheduling. That's it.

About the trampoline

  • We deliberately did NOT put the trampoline in the program. The AAP's full safety review found nets and pads do NOT reduce injuries, ages 5-14 are the highest-injury group, and a third to half of injuries happen with an adult right there.
  • If your family uses it for fun anyway: ONE jumper at a time (a smaller jumper is up to 14× more likely to get hurt), NO flips or somersaults ever, pads intact and frame at ground level, an engaged adult watching, and never when kids are tired.
  • Everything a trampoline is 'supposed' to give athletically, the pool, jump ropes, and monkey bars give more safely.

No-Equipment / Minimal-Gear Version

The whole program runs on almost nothing. Here's the swap list — a football, a few shoes as cones, a flag belt or a tucked hand-towel, a jump rope, one step or low wall, and floor space is plenty.
Kettlebells / barbell (strength load)
A loaded backpack (books), a gallon jug of water (~8 lb), or just bodyweight. Progress by slowing the tempo and adding reps — that's real strength work for this age.
Pull-up bar / monkey bars
A sturdy tree branch or a park; if none, swap in inverted rows under a table, band rows, or superman holds for the pulling/grip work.
Box (jumps/step-ups)
A porch step, curb, sturdy bench, or a low retaining wall.
Speed ladder
Chalk lines on the driveway or a row of shoes/sticks — the ladder is only a warm-up tool anyway.
Cones
Shoes, water bottles, or chalk marks.
Pool
Swap the active-recovery day for an easy bike ride, an easy trail/sidewalk walk-jog, or a mobility + stretch session.
Bike / trail loop
Any safe sidewalk, park, or open grass for the short hard runs and easy recovery movement.
PVC pipe
A broomstick or a hockey/lacrosse stick teaches the squat/overhead patterns just as well.
Bands
Bodyweight versions (push-ups, inverted rows, prisoner squats) cover the same patterns.
Nothing in the adapted version lowers the quality — the science says movement quality, short sprints, low-impact plyos, and consistency drive the gains, not fancy gear.
