Day 0 · Combine Baseline
25-30 min · before Week 1
▶
Before you train, you measure. Today is a full workout on its own: warm up completely, then run the 5-test combine at 100% effort with full rest between attempts. Write every number down — the whole 4-week program is a race against today's scores.

Warm-up

  • Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — tap for the 4-min routine

The combine (best score counts)

  • 10-yard sprint — best of 2 (phone timer; start the clock on first movement, stop as the chest crosses the line)
  • Pro-agility 5-10-5 — best of 2 (this is a sprint-touch-sprint shuttle; the Movement Library card shows the pattern)
  • Standing broad jump — best of 3 (two feet to two feet, stick the landing, measure at the heels)
  • Max push-ups in 40 seconds (chest to a fist-height, full lockout — half reps don't count)
  • Flag-belt zig-zag — max cones⇄ passed in 30 seconds through a 4-cone zig-zag wearing the flag belt
Coach for
  • Full rest between attempts — 60-90s. Tired testing = fake numbers.
  • Two tries each, best one counts. Cheer loud, time honest.

Score card (write it down!)

  • Copy this into your phone notes: 10-yd ____ s · 5-10-5 ____ s · Broad jump ____ ft-in · Push-ups ____ · Zig-zag ____ cones⇄
  • Date it. This exact card comes back out at the Retest (Week 5, Day 1).
Skill finisher (4 min): 9-Spot Hands + 10 Rapid-Catch reps — hands away from body, tuck every ball.

Cool-down

  • Cool-Down + Stretch — tap for the routine

Warm-Up & Cool-Down (every workout day)
Dynamic before · static after
▶

Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — every session, ~4 min

  • RAISE (~60s): jog the trail loop⇄ or in place + 2 build-up runs (jog → 75% → 90%). Break a light sweat.
  • ACTIVATE + MOBILIZE (~90s): 5 walking lunges w/ torso twist · 8 leg swings each way (front-back + side) · 1 inchworm walkout ×3 · 8 glute bridges · arm circles + band pull-aparts if you have one
  • POTENTIATE (~60s): 3 A-skips + 2 short accelerations (10 yd) + 2 change-of-direction cuts at speed
Why
  • Never static-stretch before speed work — it made kids 2.5% slower and cut jumps 6.3% (Paradisis 2014). Move, don't hold.

Cool-Down + Stretch — every session, ~3 min

  • ~60s easy: walk it out, easy ball toss, breathing down
  • ~90s static holds (20-30s each): quads · hamstrings · calves · hip flexors · shoulders/chest. Flexibility comes from doing this a little, often (Flexibility-window meta 2022).
Why
  • A cooldown winds you down and builds flexibility over the weeks — it does NOT 'flush' soreness (Van Hooren 2018). Static stretching belongs HERE, after.

Day 1 · Speed & Acceleration
20-25 min · Week 1
▶
Short sprints + mechanics = the #1 speed builder for your age. Kids' speed lives in the first 10-20 yards (Frontiers 2025). Don't know a drill name? Every underlined name opens a how-to card in the Movement Library.

Warm-up

  • Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — tap for the 4-min routine

Feet quicks + technical (5 min)

  • Feet quicks (super-fast little steps in place): front-back, side-side, scissors — 3 × 10s each
  • A-March → A-Skip → A-Run (the classic sprint-form ladder — tap each for a how-to), ×2 each + a 10-yd run-out
  • Arm-action: kneeling arm swings 2 × 15s (fast, relaxed hands — knees down so only the arms work)
  • Iso speed hold ×15s per side (freeze the perfect 45° launch angle — the how-to card has a video)
  • Weeks 3-4, add: claw drill ×5/leg + ankling ×10 yd (two pro sprint-form drills from the ONEighty Accelerator)

Sprint-oriented plyos (~~40 foot contacts)

  • Pogo hops 2 × 15s (straight-leg bounces off springy ankles — quiet landings)
  • Broad jump ×4 — a big two-foot jump forward; stick the landing heel-to-toe
  • Snap-downs ×5 (jump tall, then snap down and FREEZE in a low athletic stance) — this teaches stopping
Coach for
  • Land on the balls of the feet, chest tall, knees over toes
  • Grass or turf only — never concrete (plyo surface rule)

SPRINTS — 6 × 10 yd all-out sprints (sprint down, walk back, full recovery between reps)

  • These are all-out, 100% RACING sprints — not jogs, not builds
  • One sprint from each of the 6 Sprint Starts — that's your 6 reps — tap 6 Sprint Starts for how to do all six
  • Walk ALL the way back between reps (~60s per 10 yd) — every sprint starts fully rested
  • Make it a race: a sibling/parent starts a few yards ahead and your athlete chases
Coach for
  • FRESH sprints only — a tired sprint builds nothing
  • Push the ground BEHIND you, arms drive cheek-to-cheek
Skill finisher (4 min): 9-Spot Hands + 10 Rapid-Catch reps — hands away from body, tuck every ball.

Cool-down

  • Cool-Down + Stretch — tap for the routine

Day 2 · Strength A — Squat/Push/Carry
20-25 min · Week 1
▶
Technique-first strength. Pre-puberty gains are neural — coordination and force, not size (AAP 2020). Master the move, THEN add light load.

Warm-up

  • Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — tap for the 4-min routine

Squat pattern — 1-2 sets × 8-10, bodyweight/PVC⇄ only

  • PVC⇄/goblet squat to a box (squat back until you gently sit on a box or step, then stand tall — the box⇄ teaches perfect depth)
  • Progress: goblet squat holding a NO added load — pattern & bodyweight like a goblet at the chest
  • Split-squat ×8 each leg (a lunge that stays in place — one foot forward, drop the back knee toward the ground)
Coach for
  • Chest up, knees track over toes, full depth you can control
  • Barbell = an empty bar or PVC to learn the pattern ONLY — no plates in this block

Push pattern — 1-2 sets × 8-10, bodyweight/PVC⇄ only

  • Push-ups (hands on a box⇄ → floor as you get strong)
  • Band⇄ or light-KB overhead press ×8-10
  • Optional: KB floor press ×8
Coach for
  • Body in a straight line, ribs down
  • Slow down, full range — no bouncing

Carry + core (3-4 min)

  • Suitcase carry (walk 20 yd holding ONE kettlebell⇄ at your side like a suitcase) — each side
  • Farmer carry (same walk, a kettlebell⇄ in EACH hand) ×20 yd
  • Plank 2 × 20-30s + dead bug ×8/side (lie on your back, opposite arm & leg lower slowly — the how-to card shows it)
Coach for
  • Stand tall, brace the belly, don't lean
Skill finisher (4 min): Backpedal mechanics ×5 + towel/flag pulls — eyes on the belt, rip straight down.

Cool-down

  • Cool-Down + Stretch — tap for the routine

Day 3 · Engine — Circuit Game Day
18-25 min · Week 1
▶
Your CrossFit/Hyrox day — the fun one. Short work:rest circuits build real fitness in game format (School-HIIT meta 2022). Kept light so it never wrecks the speed/skill days.

Warm-up

  • Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — tap for the 4-min routine

Engine — 10-min AMRAP

  • AMRAP (as many rounds as possible) — count your rounds, beat it next week:
  • • 8 bear-crawl yards (crawl on hands and feet, knees hovering off the ground)
  • • 6 light goblet squats (NO added load — pattern & bodyweight)
  • • 4 monkey-bar traverses (or a 20s dead hang — just hang from the bar, arms straight)
  • • 20s jump rope
  • • 4 broad jumps
  • OR EMOM (every minute on the minute, rest the rest of the minute): min1 = 20s rope sprint · min2 = 6 KB deadlifts · min3 = 8 push-ups + a hang
Coach for
  • Form never breaks for a faster round — light load, clean reps
  • Score = total rounds (AMRAP) or rounds survived (EMOM). Log it, race yourself.

Trail-loop finisher (optional, 4 min)

  • 6-8 × ~20s hard runs up the 0.2-mi loop, walk back full recovery
  • This — not slow laps — is what builds a kid's engine (needs >80% effort)
Skill finisher (4 min): Towel snaps ×10 + QB 1-2-3 to 3 targets (or 15 spirals to a partner/wall).

Cool-down

  • Cool-Down + Stretch — tap for the routine

Day 4 · Active Recovery
20-25 min · Week 1
▶
Recovery is a workout — just an easy one. Easy movement clears fatigue better than a day on the couch. It sits mid-week on purpose: it separates the two hardest days from the Agility day that needs the freshest legs. No warm-up or finisher needed today.

This week: POOL⇄ DAY

  • 15-20 min EASY swim / treading water / aqua-jogging — 'easy' means they could chat the whole time (~50-60% effort)
  • Why the pool⇄: easy movement clears fatigue far better than sitting still — an easy swim cleared lactate 68% vs 20% just resting (PMC3931336), with zero impact on growing legs
  • ⚠ ADULT IN THE WATER ZONE, ARM'S REACH, ALWAYS. The pool⇄ is the #1 hazard in this whole program.

Foot, balance & eyes micro-dose (5 min)

  • Towel scrunches — 60s per foot (barefoot, scrunch a towel toward you with your toes — builds the foot's own muscles)
  • Balance + head turns — 2 × 30s per leg (stand on one leg, slowly turn your head side to side and up-down without wobbling)
  • Eyes-locked gaze drill — 2 × 20s (hold a thumb at arm's length, keep your eyes LOCKED on it while turning your head — trains steady vision on a moving field)
  • In The Athletic CheatCODE this trio is called the 'foot + balance + VOR micro-dose' — plain English: foot strength, one-leg balance, and eyes-steady-while-the-head-moves training. VOR = vestibulo-ocular reflex, the eye-steadying reflex.
  • Optional bonus: the Brock string (2 min) — a 3-bead string drill sports-vision doctors use for ball-tracking eyes; the how-to card shows the $2 DIY version.

Finish

  • 3-4 easy static stretches, 20-30s each (quads, hamstrings, calves, hips) — recovery day is the one day stretching IS the point

Day 5 · Agility & Reaction
20-25 min · Week 1
▶
Change-of-direction and reaction are MOST trainable before puberty (COD effect size 0.62 pre-PHV, Ramirez-Campillo 2023). Planned first, reactive later.

Warm-up

  • Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — tap for the 4-min routine

Lateral plyos + prep (5 min)

  • Skater bounds ×6/side (leap sideways off one leg, land on the other and STICK it — like a speed skater)
  • Lateral line hops 2 × 15s (quick two-foot hops side-to-side over a line or a stick)
  • Lateral pogos forward + back, 5 yd (springy-ankle bounces while traveling sideways)

Change of direction — planned cuts, walk-through then 75%

  • 5-5-10: 5 yd out, touch, 5 yd back, touch, 10 yd finish — both directions
  • Pro-agility with a PAUSE at each turn (teach the plant, then speed up)
  • Zig-zag flag lane: one hard cut per cone⇄, burst out of each
Coach for
  • Plant OUTSIDE your frame, hips DROP, then explode
  • Fewest steps to redirect wins (Footwork King rule)

Reaction (Wk3-4 emphasis)

  • Mirror drill: match a partner's shuffles for 5s, then chase on the break
  • Cut-on-cue: parent points or calls a direction late — cut the opposite way and go
  • Color-call sprint: 3 colored markers, sprint-touch the one that's called
Coach for
  • Read the body, not the feet — hips don't lie
  • React, don't guess
Skill finisher (4 min): Ball-security circuit (arm switches, hop-and-switch) + one Move-Menu move per rep.

Cool-down

  • Cool-Down + Stretch — tap for the routine

Day 6 · Strength B — Pull + Mobility
22-28 min · Week 1
▶
Pull-focused strength (grip = flag pulling) + a longer mobility block. Monkey bars and pull-up bar⇄ are ideal, safe, self-limiting strength for kids (AAP 2020).

Warm-up

  • Dynamic Warm-Up (RAMP) — tap for the 4-min routine

Pull pattern — 1-2 sets × 8-10, bodyweight/PVC⇄ only

  • Pull-up bar⇄ ladder: dead hangs (just hang, arms straight) → band-assisted pull-ups → negatives (jump up, then lower yourself down slowly for 5s)
  • Monkey-bar traverse — progress by distance, never by adding weight
  • Band rows ×10-12 (loop a band around a post, row the handles to your ribs, squeeze the shoulder blades)
Coach for
  • Grip and posture here = pulling flags in games
  • Soft ground under the bar, parent spotting

Single-leg + power (Wk3-4)

  • Step-ups to the box⇄ ×8/leg
  • Single-leg balance + reach ×5/leg (ankle/knee stability for cutting)
  • Wk3-4 only: 2-hand KB swing ×10 (hinge must be clean first — sub-maximal, no ballistic max)
Coach for
  • Swing is a HINGE, not a squat — snap the hips, arms are ropes

Mobility flow (~6 min)

  • World's greatest stretch ×5/side · deep squat pry-and-hold 3×20s · 90/90 hip switches ×8 · thoracic openers ×8/side · ankle wall-rocks ×10/side (from Speed Blueprint mobility)
  • PVC⇄ pass-throughs ×10 + PVC overhead squat ×8 (teaches the overhead/squat pattern — no load)
  • Optional foot micro-dose (from The Athletic CheatCODE): towel curls 60s (scrunch a towel toward you with your toes) + toe-point pogos 2×20s (springy ankle bounces) — stop at ANY heel pain (Sever's window is 9-12)
Skill finisher (4 min): Route tree in the yard — hitch/slant/out, snap the break, eyes last out.

Cool-down

Day 7 · Full Rest
0 min — that's the point · Week 1
▶
A full rest day IS training — it's when the body actually rebuilds. Nothing structured today. Seriously.

The one job today: sleep

  • SLEEP is the #1 recovery tool — target 9-12 hours/night. Young athletes sleeping ≥8h had 68% lower injury odds (Milewski). It beats every recovery gadget combined and costs nothing.
  • Play freely, hydrate, eat well. No structured workout, no 'bonus' session.
  • AAP guardrail: at least 1-2 full rest days/week; total organized-sport hours in a week should stay under the athlete's age in years.

A-March & A-Skip ▶ VIDEO
Speed drills
▶

The classic sprinter's warm-up drill. You march (then skip) forward while driving one knee up high on each step — it teaches the exact knee-punch and foot strike used in sprinting.

How to do it

  1. March forward slowly: drive your right knee up to hip height, toes pulled up, while your left arm swings forward
  2. Step down so your foot lands under your body (not out in front), then switch sides
  3. A-Skip = the same move with a little skip-hop between steps
  4. A-Run = same knee drive at a light run
Say this to your athlete
  • Knee to the sky, then hammer the foot straight DOWN
  • Stay tall — no leaning back
  • Arms swing cheek-to-cheek, elbows ~90°
Most common mistake: Landing with the foot way out in front — the foot should strike under your hips.

The 6 Sprint Starts
Speed drills
▶

Six different starting positions for your sprints. Starting from odd positions teaches you to accelerate from anywhere — exactly like reacting to a snap in a game. Use ONE start per sprint rep so you hit all six.

How to do it

  1. STANDING: normal 2-point stance, one foot slightly back
  2. FALLING-FORWARD: stand tall, lean like a falling tree, sprint when you'd tip over
  3. KNEELING: one knee down, sprint up and out on 'go'
  4. BELLY: lie face-down, hands by chest — pop up and go
  5. LATERAL: stand sideways to the direction you'll run — turn and go
  6. PUSH-UP: start at the top of a push-up — drive out
Say this to your athlete
  • First 3 steps: push the ground BEHIND you
  • Stay low coming out — rise gradually
  • Every start is a race — 100% or nothing
Most common mistake: Standing straight up on the first step instead of staying in the low 'launch' angle.
Build-Up Run
Speed drills
▶

A run that starts easy and smoothly shifts gears to fast — used in warm-ups to prepare the legs for sprinting without shocking them.

Sprint 5 yards out, touch; sprint 5 back to the start, touch; then burst 10 yards straight through. A game-speed cutting drill.

How to do it

  1. Two cones 5 yards apart, finish line 10 yards past the start
  2. Sprint to the far cone, touch the ground
  3. Sprint back to the start, touch
  4. Turn and sprint 10 yards through the finish at 100%
Say this to your athlete
  • Drop the hips into every touch
  • Fewest steps to turn around wins
  • Finish THROUGH the line, never slow early
Most common mistake: Coasting the final 10 — that burst is the whole point.

🍎 Fuel & Hydration — Parents' Corner

Everything here is adapted for OUR schedule — Tuesday/Thursday evening practices and Sunday mid-morning games — from an evidence-graded youth fuel playbook (AAP/sports-dietetics sourced) plus ONEighty's athlete nutrition guide, filtered for ages 9-12. The short version: real food, water, breakfast, snack by the clock, and 9-12 hours of sleep beat every product ever sold.
⭐ Start here: the 5 things that matter
▶
  • 1 · Real food first — meals and snacks from the grocery store beat anything in a wrapper with a mascot.
  • 2 · Water is the default drink. Water and milk cover 99% of a 9-12 year old's needs.
  • 3 · Never skip breakfast — especially Sunday. A player who didn't eat is running on fumes by the second half.
  • 4 · On game days, eat by the CLOCK, not by hunger — nerves and excitement mute a kid's appetite signals.
  • 5 · Sleep 9-12 hours. It outperforms every supplement, gadget, and powder combined, and it's free.
🍽️ The everyday plate (Mon-Sat)
▶
  • Build the plate in 3 blocks: FUEL (rice, pasta, potatoes, bread, fruit — the energy), BUILD (chicken, beef, eggs, beans, yogurt — the muscle), COLOR (vegetables & fruit — the engine oil).
  • Practice/game days: bigger FUEL block. Rest days: normal kid meals. Active boys 9-11 land around 1,800-2,200 calories on active days — but you don't need to count; the plate does the math.
  • Carbs are not the enemy — they are a 10-year-old athlete's main fuel. No dieting, no weight goals, no macro-tracking at this age. Ever.
  • Non-negotiables quietly covered by normal food: ~34 g protein/day (most kids eat 2-3× that), 1,300 mg calcium + 600 IU vitamin D (milk, yogurt, cheese — a D supplement is reasonable for Michigan Nov-Mar), 8 mg iron.
💧 Hydration by the numbers
▶
  • Daily target ages 9-12: 7-10 cups of fluid. Before play: 8-16 oz in the 1-2 hours prior. During: 3-8 oz every 15-20 min — that's every water break. After: 16-24 oz per pound lost.
  • The pee check (kid-friendly and validated): pale like lemonade = good to go · dark like apple juice = drink up. First morning pee is the most honest reading.
  • Kids feel fluid loss at just 1% of body weight (adults 2%) and their thirst signal lags — schedule drinks, don't wait for 'I'm thirsty.'
  • August practices: pre-hydrate all afternoon, freeze bottles half-full. Cold October mornings: thirst drops up to 40% in the cold but sweat doesn't stop — schedule one bottle per hour and bring something warm.
🏈 Tuesday & Thursday practice nights
▶
  • After-school snack about 1 hour before practice: carb-forward and familiar — banana + granola bar, applesauce + crackers, half a PB&J.
  • During evening practice: water only. A 90-minute practice does not earn a sports drink.
  • Dinner after practice IS the recovery plan — normal family dinner with FUEL + BUILD + COLOR. No special products needed.
  • Practice nights are the dress rehearsal: the eat-drink rhythm your player runs Tuesday is the one that will feel automatic Sunday.
🌅 The Sunday game-day timeline (mid-morning kickoff)
▶
  • SATURDAY NIGHT: carb-forward dinner (pasta night exists for a reason) · pack the bag & cooler · bottles in the freezer · normal early bedtime — the night-before sleep is the one that shows up on the field.
  • WAKE-UP (within 30 min): carb-forward, low-grease, low-fiber breakfast eaten 100 times before — pancakes/waffles, toast + banana, cereal + milk, oatmeal + berries. Can't eat early? Go liquid: smoothie or drinkable yogurt. Something ALWAYS beats nothing.
  • IN THE CAR (~1 hr before): top-off carb — banana, granola bar, or applesauce pouch + start sipping water.
  • WARM-UPS & GAME: water at every break (3-8 oz). Hot late-August kickoff or a doubleheader? THAT'S when a sports drink earns its spot.
  • AFTER (within 30-60 min): recovery snack — chocolate milk + a piece of fruit is the gold standard. Real lunch 1-2 hours later.
  • THE RULE OVER ALL RULES: nothing new on game day. New foods audition on practice nights.
🦋 The nervous stomach
▶
  • Pre-game butterflies are normal and they kill appetite. Don't force a big plate — go liquid (smoothie, drinkable yogurt, chocolate milk) and 'drink your fuel.'
  • Front-load: bigger dinner Saturday, so Sunday morning can be small.
  • Small + frequent beats one big meal: a few bites every 20-30 minutes through the morning adds up.
  • Familiar only. Comfort food is literally comforting to a nervous gut.
⏱️ Doubleheaders & jamboree days — the gap table
▶
  • Less than 45-60 min between games: quick carbs only — banana, applesauce, crackers, a few orange slices. Nothing heavy.
  • 1-2 hours between: carbs + a LITTLE protein — half sandwich, yogurt tube, cheese stick + crackers.
  • 2+ hours: a real kid-sized mini-meal — turkey sandwich, mild leftovers — plus water.
  • Eat by the clock at every gap even if 'not hungry' — the second-game fade is almost always fuel, not effort.
  • The single highest-value meal of a multi-game day: carb + protein within 30-60 min after the LAST game — that's what Monday-morning legs are made of.
🧊 The sideline cooler & team snack duty
▶
  • Cooler checklist (per ~10 kids): ½-1 gallon water backup · frozen water bottles (ice packs that become drinks) · 3-4 quick-carb options (bananas, applesauce pouches, crackers, orange slices) · 2 protein options (cheese sticks, yogurt tubes) · chocolate milk boxes for after the last game.
  • Cooler-packing pro move: freeze what can be frozen the night before; pack drinks on the bottom, squishables on top.
  • Team-snack reality check: most concession/'team snack' options are treats, not fuel. Don't ban them — time them: treats belong AFTER the last game, not between games.
  • Frame it as everyday / sometimes / after-the-last-game — never 'good foods vs bad foods.'
🥤 Sports drinks, energy drinks & supplements — straight answers
▶
  • Sports drinks: situational tools, not defaults. A 20-oz Gatorade carries ~34 g of sugar — soda territory. Earned only by long + hot + multi-game days.
  • Energy drinks: HARD NO at any age under 18. Watch the lookalike trap: Prime Hydration (a sports drink) vs Prime Energy (~200 mg caffeine — more than two coffees).
  • Protein powder: not needed at 9-12 — food covers it. Independent 2024-25 testing found ~half of powders exceeded heavy-metal safety thresholds. Creatine: no pre-teen safety data; not before puberty.
  • Pedialyte: illness-recovery tool, not a sports drink. Vitamin D in a Michigan winter: the one supplement with a real case. If a doctor ever OKs anything else, NSF Certified for Sport / Informed Sport labels only.
  • Electrolytes: kids' sweat is saltier than adults' — salty REAL food after big sweat days (pretzels, pickles, soup) beats adult electrolyte products; if you must use one, half-strength.
🔄 Recovery, honestly (and the #1 tool: sleep)
▶
  • The famous '30-minute anabolic window' is mostly overstated for a one-game week — the next 24 hours of normal meals do the real work. It matters when the next session is <24 hrs away (doubleheaders, tournament weekends).
  • The 3 R's + Rest: Refuel (carbs) · Rebuild (protein) · Rehydrate (water) · Rest. Chocolate milk is a genuinely good carb+protein package — good, not magic (much of the research was dairy-funded; it's still solid).
  • SLEEP is the recovery product: 9-12 hours for ages 9-12. Young athletes sleeping <8 hours showed ~1.7× the injury risk. Saturday night before a game is the one to protect hardest.
  • What doesn't need buying: massage guns (comfort only, parent-run, light), TENS units (skip), muscle scraping (skip at this age). See the Parents' Corner recovery-tech card below.
💬 How we talk about food, bodies & size
▶
  • Split the jobs (Satter model): parents decide WHAT/WHEN/WHERE food is offered; the kid decides WHETHER and HOW MUCH. It ends most food battles.
  • No body talk — not about your kid, other kids, or yourself in front of them. Performance words instead: strong, fast, ready.
  • Food is never a reward for winning or punishment for losing, and there are no 'good' and 'bad' foods — just everyday foods and sometimes foods.
  • Early vs late developers: at 10U/12U the size gap is a birthday-and-puberty gap, not an effort gap. Skills stick; size differences wash out. Never suggest a kid eat more/less to change their body.
  • Never appropriate at this age: detox/cleanses, fasting, keto/low-carb, weight-cutting, 'earning' food with exercise.
🥦 Picky eaters & real-life logistics
▶
  • New foods take 8-15 low-pressure exposures. Bridge from what they love (chicken nuggets → homemade breaded chicken → grilled). Smoothies hide a lot of COLOR.
  • A picky eater who eats SOMETHING beats a perfect plate that goes uneaten — especially game morning.
  • Don't share bottles — label every bottle. (Half the 'team caught a cold' stories are a shared squeeze bottle.)
  • Away-game car kit: water, two carb snacks, one protein snack, wipes. Same food rules, different parking lot.

🔧 No-Equipment / Minimal-Gear Version

The whole program runs on almost nothing. Here's the swap list — a football, a few shoes as cones, a flag belt or a tucked hand-towel, a jump rope, one step or low wall, and floor space is plenty. Orange gear chips ⇄ anywhere in the program jump straight to the matching row below.
Kettlebells / barbell (strength load)
A loaded backpack (books), a gallon jug of water (~8 lb), or just bodyweight. Progress by slowing the tempo and adding reps — that's real strength work for this age.
Pull-up bar / monkey bars
A sturdy tree branch or a park; if none, swap in inverted rows under a table, band rows, or superman holds for the pulling/grip work.
Box (jumps/step-ups)
A porch step, curb, sturdy bench, or a low retaining wall.
Speed ladder
Chalk lines on the driveway or a row of shoes/sticks — the ladder is only a warm-up tool anyway.
Cones
Shoes, water bottles, or chalk marks.
Pool
Swap the active-recovery day for an easy bike ride, an easy trail/sidewalk walk-jog, or a mobility + stretch session.
Bike / trail loop
Any safe sidewalk, park, or open grass for the short hard runs and easy recovery movement.
PVC pipe
A broomstick or a hockey/lacrosse stick teaches the squat/overhead patterns just as well.
Bands
Bodyweight versions (push-ups, inverted rows, prisoner squats) cover the same patterns.

Parents' Corner — Recovery Tech & Safety

The Recovery-Tech Truth (for parents)

  • Theragun / massage gun: OPTIONAL comfort tool. Parent-run, light setting, 1-2 min on big muscles (quads, calves, glutes) if sore. Avoid joints, spine, neck, head. No evidence it boosts youth performance — it just feels good.
  • TENS units (Chirp): SKIP for healthy kids. It's a pain-management device with zero recovery/performance evidence in healthy children. Only if a doctor prescribes it for a specific issue.
  • Muscle scraping (Sidekick/IASTM): SKIP for this age. Evidence is adults-only, low-quality, and short-lived; a growing kid with open growth plates is the wrong candidate. Clinician-only if ever.
  • What actually works: sleep, dynamic warm-ups, easy active-recovery movement, consistent post-session stretching, and not over-scheduling. That's it.

About the trampoline

  • We deliberately did NOT put the trampoline in the program. The AAP's full safety review found nets and pads do NOT reduce injuries, ages 5-14 are the highest-injury group, and a third to half of injuries happen with an adult right there.
  • If your family uses it for fun anyway: ONE jumper at a time (a smaller jumper is up to 14× more likely to get hurt), NO flips or somersaults ever, pads intact and frame at ground level, an engaged adult watching, and never when kids are tired.
  • Everything a trampoline is 'supposed' to give athletically, the pool, jump ropes, and monkey bars give more safely.
