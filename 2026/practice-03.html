High-Point 1v1 (Dog Fight) — 12 min

Two receivers 3 yards apart facing the thrower 10–12 yards away. On "Hit!" both release; coach throws a jump ball between them. Both kids attack the ball and meet it at its highest point — jump TO the ball, never wait underneath it.

  • Two hands, diamond over the face. Shield with the off arm.
  • Time the jump to the ball's peak, not your own — late jumpers win.
  • Land, spin out, tuck. Finish every rep like it's live.

Levels: Rookie — one receiver plus a non-jumping shadow, underhand lob to a spot: win the ball, no contest yet. Pro — true 1v1 jump balls, matched heights. All-Pro — back-shoulder and fade balls in a 7-yard sideline box, then the RIP round: the loser tries to rip the ball out for a 2-count after the catch.

Coach it now

  • The whole drill in three words: jump TO the ball.
  • Hands above the head, eyes through the ball — a chest catch in traffic gets ripped out.
  • Body position beats pure height. Praise the kid who stays inside the ball.

S2Defense — Pursuit Angles
Coach 2 · flags on · 2 cones for the gate
▶

Gate Pursuit (Grip & Rip) — 12 min

Two cones 5–7 yards apart make a gate. Offensive line 7 yards back, facing the gate head-on. Defensive line 5–7 yards off to the side. Runner tries to get through the gate without losing a flag; defender's job is to get to the gate first.

  • Defender leads with his body to cut the runner off and force a move — then breaks down. No lunging.
  • Eyes on the belly button / flag belt — never the head fake.
  • Two hands, grip and rip: grab the plastic piece that clips the flag into the belt. Best handle on the flag — hands don't slip off it.
  • The gate teaches the angle: run to where the ball carrier will be, not where he is.
  • If time allows, slide the offensive line left or right so the runner attacks the gate at an angle — new look, same rules.

Levels: Rookie — 7-yard gate, runner straight ahead, walk the angle at half speed first. Pro — 6-yard gate, full speed, mix in angled runs. All-Pro — 5-yard gate plus the trailer: the next kid in the offensive line releases as a trailing defender 2 beats after the runner. Lead defender delays the runner, trailer traps him from behind.

Coach it now

  • Body in front FIRST, flags second — a defender who arrives early and breaks down wins before the pull.
  • A lunge that misses is a touchdown. Patience beats speed.
  • The trailer round is the biggest lesson in flag: never give up on a play — a delayed runner is a trapped runner.

S3Defense Install — Know Your Zone
Coach 3 · large hoops, one per position · 1 ball
▶

Zone Hoops Install — 12 min

Hoops laid out on the grass, one per defensive position — each hoop is that player's zone landmark. This is where the defense learns to line up, own its grass, and move together.

  1. Walk-through: every kid stands IN his hoop. Name the position, name the zone, point to the space you own. Check the spacing between hoops.
  2. Shift with the ball: coach walks a ball around the formation — kids shift WITH it while keeping their spacing. Walk it first, then jog it.
  3. Backside rule: ball goes away from you → pursue, but do NOT chase past the middle of the field. You are the answer to the reverse and the cutback.
  4. Live: finish with a few live plays against a simple offense. Do they remember their zone when the ball is really moving?

Levels: Rookie — two hoops only, deep and short, one rule each: "this grass is yours." Pro — full hoop layout, walk then jog, coach calls the ball movement. All-Pro — full layout with live plays, backside kids graded on cutback discipline.

Coach it now

  • Spacing is the whole game — two kids in one hoop means somebody's zone is empty.
  • Eyes: check your receiver, check the QB, check back. Head on a swivel.
  • Praise the kid who STAYS home and erases the reverse — backside discipline is the hardest sell.
  • Who talks unprompted? That's your defensive captain.

Format

Two games at once so nobody stands around: Field A — the two 8U pods. Field B — the two 10/12 pods. Rotate every kid through offense and defense; borrow a kid across fields if the numbers get lopsided.

Carry the stations onto the field: on defense, call out the zones from the hoop drill before each snap — and when a runner breaks free, watch for the pursuit angle you just taught. Praise it loudly when you see it.

Let them play. Coach between snaps, not during them.

Sharks & Minnows
Whole team · flags on · the whole field
▶

How it runs

Everyone lines up on one end line with flags on. Two sharks start in the middle. On "swim!" everybody runs to the far line; anyone whose flag gets pulled becomes a shark. Last minnow standing wins.

Tonight's twist writes itself: the sharks just learned pursuit angles. Younger kids get a two-step head start each round. Run rounds until 8:00, then call it.
