Combine Tests 10-Yard Dash ▶ Pure acceleration. The number that decides flag football.

Setup

Painted lane. Cones or paint at 0 and 10 yards.

How to run it

  1. Two-point standing start, front foot on the line.
  2. Start the watch on first movement — never on a verbal 'go'.
  3. Two reps each, best time counts.
  4. Run the whole group once, then run it again. Don't do back-to-back reps.

By age group

8U10 yards
10U10 yards
12U10 yards

What to watch

  • First step forward and low, or does the kid stand straight up?
  • Arms driving front-to-back, not swinging across the body.
  • Head down for the first three steps is correct. Head up immediately means they're too tall too early.
Combine Tests Flying 10 ▶ Top speed, with the start taken out of the equation.

Setup

Run-in zone, then a 10-yard timed gate.

How to run it

  1. Kid builds through the run-in zone and hits the gate at full speed.
  2. Time only the 10 yards between the gate cones.
  3. Two reps, best counts. Full rest between — this is a max-speed test.
  4. Film it in slo-mo from the side at the gate.

By age group

8U10-yard run-in
10U15-yard run-in
12U20-yard run-in

What to watch

  • Compare to the 10-yard dash. Fast fly, slow dash = deep threat who needs a running start.
  • Fast dash, slow fly = jet sweep and short-motion kid.
  • Overstriding — reaching the foot out in front of the knee — is the most common fault and the easiest fix later.
Combine Tests Standing Broad Jump ▶ Lower-body power in one number, no equipment needed.

Setup

A takeoff line and a tape or wheel line running 10 feet out, marked every 6 inches.

How to run it

  1. Two feet behind the line, swing the arms, jump forward.
  2. Must stick the landing. Falling backward out of it doesn't count — re-do it.
  3. Measure to the back of the rearmost heel.
  4. Two reps, best counts.

By age group

8USame test, raw inches
10USame test, raw inches
12USame test, raw inches

What to watch

  • Big jump, slow dash means the power is there and the mechanics aren't. That kid gets fast with coaching.
  • Arms that don't swing are leaving 6-10 inches on the table. Note it, fix it in October.
  • Rank inside the age group only. Comparing an 8-year-old's jump to a 12-year-old's tells you nothing.
Combine Tests 5-10-5 Pro Agility ▶ The most predictive test on the field for flag football.

Setup

Three cones in a straight line, 5 yards apart. Paint the middle line.

How to run it

  1. Straddle the middle cone, hand down on the line.
  2. Sprint 5 yards right, touch the line with the hand. 10 yards left, touch. 5 yards back through the middle.
  3. Hand must touch — reaching with a foot is a re-run.
  4. One rep starting right, one starting left. Best time counts.

By age group

8U5-10-5, hand touch
10U5-10-5, hand touch
12U5-10-5, hand touch

What to watch

  • Does the kid sink his hips and plant, or round the turn like a car?
  • Eyes and head up through the change of direction, not staring at the cone.
  • Average dash time + elite agility time = corner or slot receiver. This is how you find them.
Combine Tests Reactive Mirror ▶ Tests reaction, not memorized footwork.

Setup

Two parallel cone lines (rails) 3 yards apart, each rail 4-6 yards long by age. Reactive stretch cord optional.

How to run it

  1. Kids face each other, one standing on each rail. The space between them is a river - nobody ever crosses it.
  2. The leader side-shuffles along HIS rail: start, stop, change direction, throw a fake. His feet stay on his line the whole bout.
  3. The mirror shadows from the opposite rail - square, shuffling, staying directly across like a defender locked on.
  4. 10-15 seconds, swap roles. Second bout: cord on the mirroring player so they fight resistance.

By age group

8U4-yard rails, 10 sec, light cord
10U5-yard rails, 15 sec, moderate cord
12U6-yard rails, 15 sec, full cord

What to watch

  • Reacting to the hips, or guessing off the head and shoulders?
  • Square shuffle - the feet never cross. A crossover step to catch up means he got beat.
  • The best scores here are your man-coverage defenders. Write those numbers down twice.
Hands 10-Ball Gauntlet ▶ Ten identical throws for every kid, so the scores actually compare.

Setup

One thrower who can throw, 6 balls, 4 cones, a painted sideline.

How to run it

  1. Run the same 10-throw script on every player. One point per clean catch, half a point for a body trap.
  2. 1 chest · 2 high · 3 low · 4 back shoulder · 5 over the shoulder running away
  3. 6 on the run left-to-right · 7 on the run right-to-left · 8 contested
  4. 9 sideline with both feet in · 10 coach's choice, the hardest ball this kid can catch.

By age group

8UThrows from 8 yards. #8 contested = coach waves from 3 yards off. #10 = repeat their worst throw.
10UThrows from 10 yards. #8 = coach waves at the catch point. #10 = full-extension ball.
12UThrows from 12 yards. #8 = live hand in the window. #10 = full-extension ball.

What to watch

  • Diamond above the chest, pinkies below the waist — or does everything hit the chest first?
  • Eyes stay on the ball all the way in, or look upfield early?
  • A kid who catches #5 without breaking stride runs your verticals and plays your deep safety.
  • Flinching on #8 is coachable — but you need to know about it now.
Hands Tuck & Go ▶ Secure the ball before you run, not while you run.

Setup

A catch point facing the thrower, and a cone gate 5 yards BEHIND the catch point - straight upfield, directly in line away from the thrower.

How to run it

  1. Receiver stands at the catch point, facing the coach.
  2. Coach throws from the age distance. Catch it, tuck it high and tight.
  3. Turn and sprint AWAY from the coach - upfield - straight through the gate behind you.
  4. Two reps each. Nothing is timed. You're watching whether the ball is secured BEFORE the turn.

By age group

8UThrow from 8 yds, gate 5 yds behind, tuck either arm
10UThrow from 10 yds, gate 5 yds behind, tuck to the outside arm
12UThrow from 12 yds, gate 5 yds behind, coach swipes at the ball as he turns

What to watch

  • Does the ball get secured before the first step upfield?
  • Ball carried away from the body is a fumble waiting to happen - and in flag, an easy strip.
  • Kids who look upfield before the catch are the ones who drop wide-open balls in games.
Hands 9-Spot Hands ▶ Warm-up catching that covers every place a ball can arrive.

Setup

Pairs, 8-10 yards apart.

How to run it

  1. Partner throws to nine spots: high left, high middle, high right, chest left, chest, chest right, low left, low middle, low right.
  2. Catch with the hands, tuck every single one, throw it back.
  3. Two full rounds each.

By age group

8U8 yards apart, 6 spots (drop the low corners)
10U10 yards apart, all 9 spots
12U12 yards apart, all 9 spots, one-handed on the high corners

What to watch

  • Thumbs together above the chest, pinkies together below the waist.
  • Every catch gets tucked. Every one. Habits get built in warm-up.
  • This is the fastest way to spot a body-catcher.
Hands Over-the-Shoulder Track ▶ Finding a deep ball without slowing down.

Setup

Open grass, 25-30 yards.

How to run it

  1. Kid runs away from the thrower in a straight line, looking forward.
  2. Coach calls 'ball' — kid turns his head only, finds it, catches it in stride.
  3. Alternate which shoulder he looks over.
  4. Three reps each side.

By age group

8UBall thrown 12-15 yards, high arc
10UBall thrown 18-20 yards
12UBall thrown 25-30 yards, flatter

What to watch

  • Head turns, shoulders don't. Turning the whole body kills the speed.
  • Hands come up late, not early. Early hands slow a kid down.
  • Who does this naturally? That's a vertical threat and a center fielder on defense.
Arm & QB Snap Check ▶ Centers win games in this league and nobody scouts for them.

Setup

Coach standing 5 yards back, 3 balls.

How to run it

  1. Every kid snaps. No exceptions, no volunteers-only.
  2. Three shotgun snaps to a coach 5 yards back.
  3. Three under-center snaps.
  4. Mark each one: clean, low, or wild.

By age group

8UShotgun only, coach 4 yards back
10UBoth, coach 5 yards back
12UBoth, coach 5 yards back, snap on a cadence

What to watch

  • A kid who snaps clean under zero pressure is a starter. Find two.
  • Watch the head — a center who lifts his head before the ball is gone will snap it into the ground all season.
  • Left-handed snappers spin the ball the other way. Your QB needs to know that now.
Arm & QB Target Throw ▶ Velocity and release, not lob accuracy.

Setup

Three cone gates - left, middle, right - on the same line at the age distance, gate centers 5 yards apart. A coach or parent judges from 2 yards behind whichever gate is live.

How to run it

  1. Take a snap, three-step drop, throw through the called gate.
  2. Same script for every kid: throw 1 middle, throw 2 left, throw 3 right. Throws 4 and 5: the coach calls the gate as the kid catches the snap - read and fire.
  3. Point = ball crosses between the cones in the air, no higher than the judge's head. Rainbows that drop in do not count, and neither does a one-hopper. The judge can catch it.
  4. The judge slides over behind the live gate between throws - from back there it is obvious whether the ball went through. Score X/5.

By age group

8U8 yds out, gates 5 yds wide, catch-step-throw
10U12 yds out, gates 3 yds wide, 3-step drop
12U15 yds out, gates 2 yds wide, 3-step drop

What to watch

  • Does the ball come out over the top, or push out from the ear like a shot put?
  • Any spiral at all - even a wobbly one - is a real signal at 8 years old.
  • Front shoulder pointing at the called gate before the throw starts.
  • You are hunting for two or three QB candidates. Nothing else at this station matters as much.
Arm & QB Three-Step Drop ▶ The footwork that makes everything else on time.

Setup

A line and open grass behind it.

How to run it

  1. Catch the snap, three quick steps back, plant on the back foot, throw.
  2. Ball stays at chest height the entire drop — never drops to the hip.
  3. Five reps without a ball, five with.

By age group

8UOne step back and throw. Three steps is too much for most 8U arms.
10UThree steps, no hitch
12UThree steps plus a hitch on the plant

What to watch

  • Eyes downfield the whole way, not on the feet.
  • The plant foot should stop the momentum, not drift backward.
  • A QB who drops the ball to his hip telegraphs every throw.
Arm & QB Mesh & Pitch ▶ Handoffs and pitches, both directions, every kid.

Setup

Two lines, 5 yards apart.

How to run it

  1. Two handoffs each direction: elbows up, breadbasket open, eyes on the ball.
  2. Two pitches each direction: soft, out in front, leading the runner.
  3. Every kid does both roles.

By age group

8UHandoffs only, walking speed then jog
10UHandoffs and pitches at jog speed
12UHandoffs and pitches at full speed

What to watch

  • Clean mesh hands means a running back. Note who never bobbles it.
  • Who watches the ball all the way into the pouch, and who looks upfield early?
  • A dropped pitch in a game is a turnover. Find out now who can catch one.
Running & Evasion Flag Islands (1v1) ▶ The most honest evaluation you'll run all night.

Setup

A lane. Flags on everyone.

How to run it

  1. One runner, one defender. Runner tries to cross the far line, defender tries to pull a flag.
  2. Next pair goes the instant a rep ends. Lines never get longer than four kids.
  3. Three reps on offense, three on defense, for everyone.
  4. Record wins and losses in both roles.

By age group

8U8 × 12-yard lane, defender 5 yards off
10U10 × 15-yard lane, defender 5 yards off
12U12 × 18-yard lane, defender 3 yards off, coach throws the ball in on rep 3

What to watch

  • Offense: one move and go, or a dancer who runs out of room?
  • Defense: breakdown and shuffle, or lunge and whiff? Eyes on the hips, not the head fake.
  • Guarding the flags with a hand is illegal. Note who does it — teach it later.
  • The kid who wins on effort rather than talent is your captain. Circle that number.
Running & Evasion Jet Start ▶ Speed in space, from motion.

Setup

A line of scrimmage and a motion path.

How to run it

  1. Kid starts in motion parallel to the line, full speed.
  2. Takes the handoff or pitch on the move, gets vertical immediately.
  3. Three reps each direction.

By age group

8UWalk the motion, jog the exchange
10UFull-speed motion, handoff
12UFull-speed motion, handoff or pitch, defender chases

What to watch

  • Does the kid slow down to take the ball? That kills the whole play.
  • One cut and go vertical. Bouncing it outside forever is how jet sweeps die.
  • Your best 10-yard dash times belong here.
Running & Evasion Cut & Burst ▶ Change direction without losing speed.

Setup

Cones at 5 and 10 yards, offset 3 yards.

How to run it

  1. Sprint to the first cone, plant the outside foot, cut, sprint to the second.
  2. Two reps each direction.
  3. The cut is one step. Two steps is a gather, and a gather gets your flag pulled.

By age group

8UCones 3 yards offset, jog-to-sprint
10UCones 3 yards offset, full speed
12UCones 4 yards offset, full speed, coach points the direction late

What to watch

  • Foot plants outside the body, hips drop, shoulder turns first.
  • Rounded cuts mean the kid is scared of the plant. Not a talent problem.
  • Compare to the 5-10-5. They should agree. If they don't, watch again.
Defense Flag Pull Technique ▶ The one defensive skill that matters more than all the others.

Setup

Pairs, 5 yards apart, flags on.

How to run it

  1. Defender breaks down: feet wide, knees bent, hands out and low at hip height.
  2. Eyes on the belt buckle. Not the ball, not the shoulders, not the face.
  3. Runner jogs past at half speed; defender pulls one flag cleanly.
  4. Five reps, then build to full speed.

By age group

8UHalf speed only, straight-line runner
10UBuild to three-quarter speed, one cut allowed
12UFull speed, runner can use any move

What to watch

  • Hands low and in front. Reaching down from above is how kids miss.
  • One flag. Grabbing the shirt or the waist is a penalty and a habit.
  • Breakdown before the pull. Kids who run through the pull miss and give up the big play.
Defense Backpedal & Break ▶ Cover deep, then close fast.

Setup

A line and cones at 10 yards.

How to run it

  1. Backpedal on the whistle, chest over the toes, short choppy steps.
  2. On the second whistle, plant and break forward at 45 degrees.
  3. Alternate break direction. Six reps.

By age group

8U5-yard backpedal, break to a cone
10U8-yard backpedal, break on the coach's point
12U10-yard backpedal, break to a thrown ball

What to watch

  • Chest stays over the toes. Standing straight up means they fall backward.
  • The break is one plant step, not a turn-and-run.
  • Who does this comfortably? That's your safety.
Defense Zone Drop Landmarks ▶ Everybody covers grass, nobody chases the ball.

Setup

Painted or coned landmarks on the field.

How to run it

  1. Each defender walks to his spot on the call. Walk it before you run it.
  2. Then jog it. Then do it live against air.
  3. Coach stands at QB and points; defenders drop and find the ball with their eyes.

By age group

8UTwo zones only — deep and short. Keep it that simple.
10UThree zones, walk then jog
12UFull zone spacing, live drop with eyes on the QB

What to watch

  • Who drifts toward the ball instead of holding the spot? That instinct is good and bad — you need to know.
  • Head on a swivel: check the receiver, check the QB, check back.
  • Who talks unprompted? That's your defensive captain.
Defense Pursuit Angles ▶ Take the angle, not the chase.

Setup

Wide open grass, a runner and two pursuers.

How to run it

  1. Runner takes off across the field. Pursuers must take an angle to cut him off, not run at where he is.
  2. Three reps each role.
  3. If the pursuer ends up behind the runner, the angle was wrong. Say so and re-run it.

By age group

8UHalf field, runner at three-quarter speed
10UHalf field, runner full speed
12UFull field, runner full speed, one blocker

What to watch

  • Running at the ball carrier's current position is the most common youth defensive mistake.
  • Head up, eyes on the hips, feet aimed at the spot ahead of him.
  • This drill wins games in flag football and almost nobody runs it.
Speed & Agility Speed Ladder Series ▶ Foot speed and rhythm — and a great thing for kids to do while waiting.

Setup

One speed ladder.

How to run it

  1. One foot in each · two feet in each · lateral two-in · in-in-out-out · Icky shuffle.
  2. Two passes of each pattern.
  3. Fast feet, quiet feet, eyes up.

By age group

8UOne foot in, two feet in, lateral two-in
10UAdd in-in-out-out
12UAll five patterns, then repeat the whole series for time

What to watch

  • Eyes up. A kid staring at the ladder learns nothing transferable.
  • Quiet feet — stomping means the ground contact is too long.
  • Rhythmic or clunky? Clunky at 12 is worth a note; clunky at 8 is just being 8.
Speed & Agility A-Skip ▶ Teaches the running position without saying a word about it.

Setup

20 yards of open grass.

How to run it

  1. Skip forward driving one knee up to hip height, toe pulled up, opposite arm driving.
  2. Land on the ball of the foot underneath the body.
  3. Two passes of 20 yards.

By age group

8U10 yards, march it first
10U20 yards
12U20 yards, then a 20-yard A-skip into a 10-yard sprint

What to watch

  • Foot lands underneath the hip, not out in front.
  • Toe pulled up toward the shin — that's the whole drill.
  • Kids who can't A-skip usually overstride when they sprint.
Speed & Agility Acceleration Position ▶ The first three steps, taught standing still.

Setup

A wall, a fence, or a partner.

How to run it

  1. Lean into the wall at about 45 degrees, arms straight, body in one line from heel to head.
  2. Drive one knee up, hold three seconds. Switch.
  3. Then three rapid switches. Then five.
  4. Body angle never changes. If the hips sag, reset.

By age group

8UHold position only, no switches
10USingle switches and three-count
12URapid switches, five-count, then release into a 10-yard sprint

What to watch

  • One straight line from the back heel through the head.
  • Hips forward. A sagging middle is the whole reason kids stand up out of a start.
  • This is the single best thing you can do for a slow kid, and it costs nothing.
Speed & Agility Resisted Starts ▶ Force the body into the right angle using the cord.

Setup

Reactive stretch cord, coach or partner anchoring.

How to run it

  1. Belt on, cord anchored behind. Kid drives forward against the resistance for 10 yards.
  2. Three reps. Then take the cord off and run one free — that one feels like flying.
  3. Rest fully between reps. This is a power drill, not conditioning.

By age group

8ULight resistance, coach walks forward with them
10UModerate resistance
12UFull resistance, coach anchors and holds

What to watch

  • The resistance does the teaching — you shouldn't have to say much.
  • Watch the shin angle on the first three steps. It should point forward, not up.
  • The free rep after is the point. Kids feel the difference and it sticks.
Warm-Up Dynamic Line Series ▶ The same warm-up every practice, all season, until they lead it themselves.

Setup

Half the field, 20 yards down and jog back.

How to run it

  1. Knee hugs · quad pull with a reach · walking lunge · side lunge · Frankenstein
  2. High knees · butt kickers · A-skip · side shuffle both ways · carioca both ways · backpedal
  3. Build-ups at 50%, 75%, 100%.
  4. Coaches lead it for the first two weeks. Captains lead it after that.

By age group

8U15 yards, skip the carioca until week 3
10U20 yards, full series
12U20 yards, full series, captains lead by week 3

What to watch

  • Evaluation starts in the warm-up, not at the first station.
  • Who can carioca without thinking? Hips-and-feet kid — corner or slot.
  • Who competes on a build-up when nobody said it was a race?
