3x3 Zone Install (Six Zones)

All six zones of the 3x3 base defense marked with cones — three underneath, three deep.

  • Know your zone and your responsibility — every kid says his out loud: "this grass is mine."
  • The HELP rule: when the ball goes to someone else's zone, you have an aiming point — underneath help aims for the runner's escape lane, deep help caps him deep. Help arrives under control, never past the ball.
  • Walk a ball around the formation; everyone shifts and re-calls their landmark. Finish with slow-motion plays — who has him, who's help?

Coach it now

  • A kid who can't SAY his responsibility doesn't know it. Ask every kid.
  • Help is a lane, not a stampede — aim where the runner is going.

DB School: Creep, Pedal & Weave

Review the base first, then chain the weave:

  1. "Creep" — slow controlled walk back.
  2. "Pedal" — lengthen the stride and "run" backwards. Hips down, chest over knees.
  3. Crossover Weave — line up perpendicular to the WR, then: crossover shuffle back → crossover run back (front foot arch to back foot toe) → coach points L or R: move at a 45° angle — front shoulder down, shoulders & hips open and pointing at the receiver, chin to chest, squeeze to the receiver's hip.
  4. Stay over the top of the WR as long as you can — about an arm's length of cushion.
  5. Finish: coach throws it deep. Sprint back in the weave stance — do NOT open up into a flat backpedal — then go high-point the ball.

Coach it now

  • Hips down, chest over knees — a tall pedal can't react.
  • Front foot arch to back foot toe on the crossover. Feet never tangle.
  • On the deep ball: weave-sprint, never flat backpedal — attack the ball in the air.

1-on-1 Route Duel (WR vs DB)

Once everyone has good DB School reps, add a WR for live 1-on-1. Pair kids of similar skill and speed — a mismatch teaches nobody anything.

  • Routes: go · dig · out · curl · comeback.
  • WRs: attack the DB's opposite hip, maintain or create separation — and remember: it's not enough to be fast, you have to stop on a dime and change direction.
  • The sell: drive the route hard toward the opposite direction, get the DB to flip his hips and commit — THEN cut and create separation.
  • DBs: everything from the backpedal drill is live here — cushion, weave, patience. Don't flip until the route makes you.

Coach it now

  • Sell it with the eyes and shoulders, not just the feet.
  • Separation comes from the STOP, not the sprint.
  • Re-pair the moment one kid wins three straight.

Gate Pursuit (Grip & Rip) — coach's choice with Flag-Pull Angle Progression

Beat the runner to the spot: run to where he will be, not where he is. Body in front first, break down — no lunging — then two hands to the flag, grip and rip the plastic clip.

If the gate looks sharp, graduate to the three-stage progression: tunnel → zig-zag → open-field square.

Coach it now

  • A lunge that misses is a touchdown — patience beats speed.
  • Both hands, every pull.

Handoff Weave Relay

  1. Two equal lines facing each other — Line A and Line B. First kid in Line A has the ball.
  2. Staying to the left, first kid in A and first kid in B run at each other; A hands the ball off to B.
  3. B secures it, keeps running, and hands it to the next kid coming from Line A.
  4. That kid secures and carries it to the next Line B player... and on, and on.
  5. Count the streak out loud — how many clean handoffs in a row without a drop? Beat the record every round.

Coach it now

  • Secure, tuck, THEN run — the streak dies on kids who sprint before the ball is tucked.
  • Stay to the left every time — the lane discipline IS the drill.

Paper to Grass (Run What You See)

Show the route on paper → the kid lines himself up to match the picture — right side of the ball, right depth — → on "Hike!" he runs exactly what he saw, full speed, cut where the paper put it.

  • First reps: cones mark the break point. Then take the cones away — the picture lives in his head.
  • Keep cards simple early (go, slant, out); rotate a new card and a new kid every rep.

Coach it now

  • Lining up right is half the play — wrong side of the ball kills the route before the snap.
  • Full speed even off a card — a walked route teaches walking.

Format

Field A — 8U. Field B — 10/12, red vs blue straight from the drills. Rotate every kid through offense and defense.

Carry the drills onto the field: on 10/12 defense, call the six zones before the snap and grade the help lanes; watch the DBs weave instead of flat-pedaling. On 8U, praise the clean mesh and the kid who lines up right without being told.

Let them play. Coach between snaps, not during them.
