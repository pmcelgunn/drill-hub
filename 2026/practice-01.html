Field Setup
Patrick + first coach to arrive · spray paint, measuring wheel, cones
▶

Paint these lines first — they never move all season

  • Speed lane: a straight 40-yard lane. Paint hash marks at 0, 10, 20, 30, 40. That one lane serves the 10-yard dash and every fly variation for all three ages.
  • Jump box: a takeoff line with a measuring tape or wheel line running 10 feet out, marked every 6 inches.
  • Agility square: three cones in a straight line, 5 yards apart (the 5-10-5). Paint the center line so the cone can't drift.

Then set the four stations

Stations go in a wide square with the middle open — that middle becomes the bracket field at 7:18 without moving a single cone. See the Field Map for measured spacing.

Check-in table

One table at the parking-lot end. Numbered pinnies laid out in order, master roster card, spare tape and a marker.

Pinnie numbers. You have 23 pinnies (11 red + 12 blue). Tape a number 1–23 on each one tonight before you leave the house. The number is the kid's combine ID for the entire evening — coaches write numbers on cards, never names. If 24+ show up, tape a number directly on the shirt.

The 20-second intake

  1. Kid gives name and age group.
  2. You hand them the next numbered pinnie in the stack and write number, name, age group on the master roster card.
  3. Flag belt on. Pinnie on over the shirt.
  4. Send them to the open grass — ball in hand, play catch until the horn.

Pod sort — do this at 6:08, not before

Once you can see who actually showed, split into four pods of roughly equal size. Sort by age group first; only split an age group if it's bigger than seven kids.

If turnout is lopsided

  • One age dominates — split that age into two pods and put the two small groups together as one mixed pod. Mixed pods are fine; the tests scale on the card.
  • Fewer than 16 kids — go to three pods and 18-minute rotations. Drop the Reactive Mirror from Station 2.
  • More than 28 kids — five pods, 11-minute rotations, and Station 3 runs two throwers at once.
Only 3 adults? Drop Station 2 and fold the 5-10-5 into Station 1 as a fourth test. Three stations, 18 minutes each, same finish time. You lose the Reactive Mirror score and nothing else.
Warm-up

Dynamic Warm-Up
Whole group, one big line · coaches lead from the front
▶

Circle up — 3 min

Arm circles forward and back · over the shoulder · across the chest · fingers pulled back · wrist rolls. Names go around the circle while you do it: every kid says their name and their favorite team.

Line drills — 9 min, 20 yards down, jog back

Knee hugs · quad pull with a reach · walking lunge · side lunge · Frankenstein · high knees · butt kickers · A-skip · side shuffle both ways · carioca both ways · backpedal · build-ups at 50%, 75%, 100%.

The evaluation starts here — watch, don't coach

  • Who can carioca without thinking about it? That's a hips-and-feet kid — corner or slot.
  • Who backpedals smoothly instead of falling backward? That's your safety.
  • Who competes on the 100% build-up when nobody said it was a race?
  • Who is already talking to teammates by minute six?
Do not fix technique tonight. If a kid runs with crossed arms or overstrides, write it down and say nothing. Coaching cues change the numbers you're about to record, and then you have no baseline.

S1Speed & Power
Coach: Patrick · painted 40-yd lane, jump box, two timers, phone on slo-mo
▶

0:00–5:00 · 10-Yard Dash — 2 reps, best counts

Standing two-point start, foot on the line. Start the watch on first movement, not on a "go." Run the whole pod through, then run it again. Same distance for every age — 10 yards is the number that decides flag football.

5:00–10:00 · Flying 10 — 2 reps, best counts

Build up through a run-in zone, then time only the last 10 yards. This is top-end speed, separate from the start. Phone on slo-mo at the fly gate.

10:00–14:00 · Standing Broad Jump — 2 reps, best counts

Two feet on the line, swing and jump, stick the landing. Measure to the back of the heel. A jump they fall backward out of doesn't count — make them re-do it.

Chart it (1–5) — Start Quality

  • First step forward and low, or stands straight up out of the start?
  • Arms driving front-to-back, or swinging across the body?
  • Does the fly time beat the dash time by a lot? That kid is a deep threat who needs a running start — put him at Z, not in the backfield.
  • Dash fast but fly slow? That's a jet-sweep and short-motion kid.

S2Agility & Change of Direction
Coach 2 · painted 5-10-5, reactive stretch cord, timer
▶

0:00–8:00 · 5-10-5 Pro Agility — 2 reps, best counts

Straddle the middle cone. Sprint 5 yards right, touch the line, 10 yards left, touch, 5 yards back through the middle. Hand must touch the line — no reaching with a foot. Run one rep starting right, one starting left, and record the better time.

This is the single most predictive test on the field for flag football. A kid who is average in the 10-yard dash and elite here is a corner or a slot receiver.

8:00–14:00 · Reactive Mirror — 2 bouts of 15 seconds, scored 1–5

Two kids face each other across a 5-yard box. One leads, one mirrors. No ball, no whistle, just react. Swap roles. Then run it once with the reactive stretch cord on the mirroring player so they fight resistance while changing direction.

0:00–9:00 · The Gauntlet — same 10 throws for every kid, scored X/10

Run the identical script on every player so the scores actually compare. One point per clean catch. A trap against the body is half a point — write it as 0.5.

  1. Chest, standing still
  2. High — above the head
  3. Low — below the waist
  4. Back shoulder — they have to turn to find it
  5. Over the shoulder, running away
  6. On the run, left to right
  7. On the run, right to left
  8. Contested — a coach waves a hand through the catch window
  9. Sideline — both feet down inside the paint
  10. Coach's choice — the hardest ball you think this kid can catch

9:00–14:00 · Tuck & Go

Catch, tuck, and sprint through a cone gate 5 yards away. Two reps. You're timing nothing — you're watching whether the ball gets secured before the kid starts running.

S4Arm & Mesh
Coach 4 · 6 balls, 2 cones for a line, target cones, measuring wheel
▶

0:00–4:00 · Snap Check — pass / fail, both kinds

Every single kid snaps. Three shotgun snaps to a coach standing 5 yards back, three under-center snaps. Mark each as clean, low, or wild. Centers win games in this league and nobody scouts for them. Find yours tonight.

4:00–9:00 · Target Throw — 5 attempts, scored X/5

Take a snap, three-step drop, throw through a cone gate. One point per ball through the gate on a line. A rainbow that drops through doesn't count — this is about velocity and release, not lob accuracy.

9:00–12:00 · Distance Throw — best of 2, measured

Three-step run-up allowed. Measure where it lands with the wheel. Record the raw number.

12:00–14:00 · Mesh & Pitch

Two handoffs and two pitches each, both directions. Watch the breadbasket: elbows up on the handoff, eyes on the ball through the pitch.

Flag Islands Bracket
Whole group in the middle · all coaches · flags on everyone
▶

The format

Two lanes running at once, side by side. One runner, one defender. The runner tries to cross the far line; the defender tries to pull a flag. The instant a rep ends the next pair goes — no standing around, no lines longer than four kids.

Every kid gets three reps on offense and three on defense. Record wins and losses in both roles. That win-loss record is the most honest number you'll collect all night, because the kid is trying to win, not trying to test well.

Matchmaking

Age against age for the first two rounds. Round three, let the winners cross ages — an 8U kid who beats a 12U kid is information you want, and the kids will lose their minds. Everybody watches round three.

Chart it (1–5) — both roles

  • Offense: one move and go, or a dancer who runs out of room? Does he protect the flags with his hands (illegal, teach it later, note it now)?
  • Defense: breakdown and shuffle, or lunge and whiff? Eyes on the hips or eyes on the head fake?
  • Who pulls flags clean when it matters? Those are your corners and your rusher.
  • Who wins with effort rather than talent? Write that number down and circle it. That kid is your captain.

Team Relay Finish
Whole group · pinnie colors become teams · speed ladder + cones
▶

Red vs Blue, mixed ages, loudest eight minutes of the night

Ladder in, sprint to the cone, catch a ball from a coach, sprint back, tag the next runner. Ages stagger their start line so it's actually a fair race: 8U starts 5 yards ahead, 10U at 3, 12U at the line.

Two heats. No scoring, no cards, no evaluation. This one is for them.

Why this is here

Ninety minutes of being measured is a lot to ask of an eight-year-old. The last thing they do before the huddle should be a game they won as part of a team, so the memory they take home is "that was fun," not "I got timed."

Whole group, knee down, coaches standing
▶

Walk and stretch — 4 min

Easy lap, then quads, hamstrings, calves, hip flexors, shoulders. Water.

The talk — 5 min

  • Name three kids out loud for something specific they did tonight. Not "good job" — the actual thing.
  • Tell them what tonight was: we measured a starting point, and every one of these numbers gets re-tested at the end of the season. The award nobody can buy is Most Improved.
  • Tell them what's next: positions get announced at Practice 2, and nothing tonight is permanent.

Break it — 2 min

Everyone in, hand in the middle. Pick the break word tonight and keep it all season.

Say this out loud before they leave: nobody's time is getting posted with their name attached. Kids compare numbers and eight-year-olds do not need a public ranking on night one. Leaderboards go up by age group at Practice 3, and only for personal records.
