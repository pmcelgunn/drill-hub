Crisp Routes (Cone Cuts) — 12 min

Cones mark the release, the cut point and the finish of every route so the kids can see the path and the distances. Start simple, get progressively harder — routes are the coach's call.

Stance first, every rep: front foot is the inside foot (closest to the ball), toe on the line or just behind, athletic stance, hands and arms twitchy — not resting on a knee.

  • Fast release on "Hike!" — full speed to the cut point. Sell the first half of the route so the DB commits.
  • At the cone: break down, drop the hips, drive through the plant and push the ground away.
  • Head around quickly, eyes up, look for the ball immediately.
  • Hands and arms keep pumping until the last second — then go make the grab.

Route examples by level: Rookie — hitch (5 yards and turn) and slant; one cut, walked first, then full speed. Pro — add out, in and curl; full speed off the line, catch on the move. All-Pro — add post, corner, comeback and double moves (hitch-and-go, out-and-up); sell the first move hard enough that a DB bites.

Coach it now

  • Rounded cuts are the enemy — the cut should look like a corner, not a curve.
  • Slowing down before the break tells the DB where you're going. Full speed into the cone.
  • Fix the stance before the snap: inside foot forward, hands off the knees, twitchy.

S2Defense — DB Backpedal & Break
Coach 2 · 1 ball · flags on · 1 to 4 DBs at a time
▶

DB Backpedal, Swivel & Break — ~8 min

1 to 4 DBs line up in a correct corner's stance facing the coach, who holds a football. On "Hike!" they dropstep and backpedal — proper technique and body position so they're efficient, fast and ready to spring.

  • As they pedal, the coach points left, right or straight back. DBs swivel their hips that way and keep moving, still in the backpedal.
  • Footwork on the swivel has to be crisp — plant, flip the hips, no crossed feet, no tripping over themselves.
  • At the coach's discretion: "BALL!" — he throws it to one DB, anywhere. That DB breaks out of the pedal, reacts fast, and goes to intercept it.

Wrinkle: "RUN!" — ~4 min, after everyone has reps

Same drill, different ending. Instead of "Ball!", the coach yells "RUN!" and hands the ball to the next kid in line. The DBs break out of the pedal and come pull the runner's flags. This is the delayed handoff, the QB sneak, or the scramble when the 5-second pass clock runs out — leave your assignment and go make the play on the ball carrier.

Levels: Rookie — 1–2 DBs at a time, points left/right only, soft close throws; add "Run!" only if the pedal is clean. Pro — 3–4 at a time, all three directions, ball anywhere plus the "Run!" wrinkle. All-Pro — 4 at a time, rapid-fire points, ball thrown to the far DB for a long break, runner gets one move on "Run!"

Coach it now

  • Chest over the toes in the pedal — a kid who stands tall can't break on anything.
  • Hips flip, feet don't cross. Watch the feet on every point.
  • On "Ball!" — attack it. Waiting underneath it loses to the receiver in a game.
  • On "Run!" — reaction time IS the drill. Who leaves his pedal first? That's your closer.

Offense Line-Up Install — 12 min

Walk the kids through exactly how to line up at every offensive position before the snap — spacing, foot placement, hands, arms. Then do it all again from the other formation.

  • QB — under center: hands under the center's hips, feet shoulder-width, knees bent, eyes up. Shotgun: 5 yards back, hands up as a target, same knees, same eyes.
  • Center: over the ball, feet wide, ball ready to snap — and athletic, because he's eligible in our league and releases into a route after the snap.
  • WRs: inside foot forward, toe on the line or just behind, hands twitchy, proper split from the ball (cones mark it).
  • RB: beside or behind the QB depending on the formation, athletic stance, eyes on the ball, ready for the mesh.
  • Walk it under center, check every kid. Shift to shotgun, check again. Finish with mock snaps — "Down, set, hike!" — from both.

Levels: Rookie — two positions at a time, under center only. Pro — full offense, both formations, kids call their own alignment before the coach checks. All-Pro — both formations plus motion: one WR shifts before the snap and everyone re-checks spacing.

Coach it now

  • Every kid should be able to say where he lines up and why. Ask them.
  • Spacing is the whole picture — WRs too tight means every route ends up in the same grass.
  • Under center vs shotgun changes the QB and RB the most. Watch those two on the switch.

Format

Two games at once so nobody stands around: Field A — the two 8U pods. Field B — the two 10/12 pods. Rotate every kid through offense and defense; borrow a kid across fields if the numbers get lopsided.

Carry the stations onto the field: before every snap, check the line-up — inside foot, hands off knees, splits. On defense, watch the DBs' pedal and who breaks first on a scramble. Praise the clean cut and the early break loudly when you see them.

Let them play. Coach between snaps, not during them.
